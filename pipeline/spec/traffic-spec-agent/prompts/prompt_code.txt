## The gate is not yours to reconfigure

Run it exactly as shown above — the real `--task-dir`, and `--library-root` pointing at the real library. Specifically, do not:

- pass a `--library-root` that is a copy of the library, however faithful,
- edit, regenerate or replace `baseline.json`,
- delete or move a file the gate names, to make the comparison come out clean,
- pass `--skip-build`.

Each of those changes what the gate measures rather than what it is measuring, and the harness re-checks scope after your session against its own record of the library. A verdict obtained that way is rejected as `GATE_BYPASS` and the node is thrown away — so it cannot help you, and it destroys the one signal that says your rule is sound.

If the gate seems wrong, that is worth reporting and is sometimes true: write what you observed in `{node_report_file}`, set `blocked`, and stop. A correct rule with an honest blocker gets re-planned and lands next iteration. A rule that passed by reconfiguring its own test does not.

## Reading a `SCENARIO_FAIL`

The gate prints, per grounding, a per-frame bitmap of where the grounded formula holds (position 0 leftmost, `1` = holds). Two patterns account for most failures:

- **all zeros** — the formula is false everywhere. Usually an inverted relation argument order. `rel a lbl b` means "**a** is `lbl` **b**": `Pred.behind v w` says v is behind w, so in a following rule *v is the follower*. Check that your antecedent is not describing the opposite vehicle.
- **all ones** — the formula is true everywhere, so the antecedent never fires. Usually a proximity band that does not match the trace, or an attribute read on a non-ego vehicle.

Then check the scenario trace itself. A negative scenario that fails to violate the rule is as often a trace that does not actually depict the forbidden conduct as it is a wrong formula.

## Traps specific to this domain

- **Proximity band names do not mean what they say in English.** `near` is the 10–16 m band, the second *farthest*. `safetyHazard` is the *closest* (<2 m). For "within N metres" always use `Pred.withinMetres N`, never a raw band.
- **The five dynamics channels — `speed`, `accel`, `throttle`, `brake`, `steer` — exist on the ego vehicle only.** A speed predicate on a non-ego binder is always false, which silently makes your rule vacuous. Gate such rules on `Pred.isEgo`.
- **Numeric attributes are decimal strings.** Never compare them with `attrEq`; use `Pred.speedLe`, `Pred.speedLeKph`, `Pred.attrMilliLe`, etc. Bounds are milli-units: 1 m/s is `1000`.
- **`isIn` is overloaded** across entity→lane, lane→road, and road→junction. Use the structural predicates (`Pred.inLane`, `Pred.inJunction`, `Pred.approachingJunction`) rather than raw `isIn`.
- **Strong-next semantics.** `.bounded n φ` requires `n` in-bounds frames; running off the end of the trace is false. Give scenario traces enough frames for the window you use.
- **Distance and angular relations hold between the ego vehicle and others** in default captures. A rule relating two non-ego actors by proximity may never ground on real data.

## Your report

When the gate prints `OK`, or when you must stop because of a blocker, write `{node_report_file}`:

```json
{{"node_id": "{node_id}",
  "status": "ok",
  "formalization_choices": ["three-frame persistence threshold for 'persistently'"],
  "blockers": [],
  "missing_vocab": [],
  "suggest_decompose": []}}
```

- `status`: `"ok"` or `"blocked"`.
- `formalization_choices`: every reading you had to pick that the text did not settle. This is how a human audits your work.
- `blockers`: why you stopped, if you did. Name the missing scene-graph field for a `CARLA_INCOMPATIBLE` stop.
- `missing_vocab`: predicates you needed that do not exist, described precisely enough for the plan agent to specify them.
- `suggest_decompose`: if this clause is really several obligations, name them. The plan agent will split it.

An honest `blocked` report is a better outcome than a rule that passes by weakening what the law says. Do not weaken a scenario, or drop a conjunct, to make the gate pass.
