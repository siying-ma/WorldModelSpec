## Steering-angle decision (1.6)

`kin.steer_angle` will represent the arithmetic mean of measured left and right front-wheel angles, in degrees using the existing Core sign convention (positive left, negative right). Two-wheel vehicles use their single measured front wheel. For example, Core-oriented `[25°,20°]` gives `+22.5°`; the mirrored `[-20°,-25°]` gives `-22.5°`.

Record both raw values and wheel identifiers in source evidence. The mean is a declared summary, not a single-wheel-model equivalent angle. Preserve legacy front-left measurements under their original method; never relabel them as means. Unsupported layouts or missing required measurements are reported explicitly. Recordings accepted under the new complete-steering profile must have the required angle at every applicable actor/frame; pedestrians are not applicable. Implementation, migration and failure tests are specified in handoff section 1.6.

## Polygon repair and curved centre-line plan (2.5 / 3.3)

Crosswalk repair removes a redundant repeated closing vertex, then validates the actual polygon in Core. Do not assume every invalid shape has only that defect.

For centre lines, the proposed interim convention is a **1 cm total-width symmetric strip: 0.5 cm (0.005 m in world coordinates) on each side** of the retained line. This is estimated geometry, not measured paint width. Where a verified physical marking outline exists, preserve that source-backed outline instead. Consumer agreement on the line convention remains required before publication; this documentation does not change the ontology or Core.

In the manifest's atoms, declare `"st.region_geometry": "estimate:mapped_geometry_with_centreline_buffer_1cm"`, using the supported manifest serialization after parser validation. Here `1cm` means **total width**, not offset on each side. Record total width 0.01 m, half-width 0.005 m, endpoint/corner handling and curve-sampling conventions. A shared path-level declaration does not imply that every region is buffered; retain each region's actual source/method. Preserve measured marking boundaries unchanged. Where only a geometric centre line is known, the strip estimates its geometry for Core and establishes neither paint presence nor paint width. This exception is for centre lines, not a blanket buffer for stop lines or other missing boundaries.

For a curve, use one continuous polygon per genuinely connected, non-branching centre line, with consistent joins and endpoints. Do not create a new structure for each sampled segment. Remove redundant collinear points and sample adaptively: tight curves need more vertices, gentle curves fewer. Declare and validate maximum geometric approximation error; the 0.5 cm half-width does not bound curve-sampling error. Approximately N centre-line samples produce 2N boundary vertices, with additional join vertices depending on construction.

This avoids the old B1 bug of hundreds of overlapping property-carrying roadway structures, but does not guarantee cheap execution: Core's self-intersection check compares edge pairs, with quadratic worst-case work. Before batch conversion, benchmark actual evaluator execution on representative straight, long, curved and sharply bent examples. Freeze a tested accuracy/runtime profile and report vertex counts and results. No runtime claim has yet been measured. Failed geometry or performance remains explicitly blocked; never silently coarsen it, substitute a convex hull, create hundreds of tiny regions or fall back to distance to a reference point. Rebuild affected historical exports offline where saved coordinates suffice, with normal validation and provenance; no automatic GPU rerun.

## Sight distance: ego reference, estimate method and universal acquisition

The revised `st.sight_distance` atom uses the ego's measured body as the reference target at its evaluated road location and hypothetical approaching viewpoints sampled down the road.
