## Purpose

The training set builds the autoformalization repository: its ontology, derived concepts, plans and rules. The test set stands for legislation the repository has never seen — a road structure with its own traffic pattern, a road-user class and its device, new vehicle states — and asks whether the pipeline notices that the ontology lacks an atom the new text needs (a person then decides to add it), and formalizes the text once the atom is added. Controls, which need only training atoms, test that it does not ask for an atom a law does not need.

## Limits of the claim

- **Atoms only.** No test line needs a formula operator the training Core lacks; whether the pipeline notices a missing operator is not measured.
- **Seven families.** Results are reported per family, not per line. The families a simulator shows now (roundabout, lighting, reverse lamp, the grade) are judged end to end; the others on formalization until their assets exist.
- **Visible novelty.** The missing atom is named in the text (a structure, a class, a lamp). Atoms whose need is hidden in ordinary words and can be computed from training atoms were not held out: for them the right answer is a derived concept, not a gap.
- **Scope.** The corpus covers ordinary urban Las Vegas driving with a vehicle driver as the party judged. Laws triggered by a rare actor or event (funeral processions, ice-cream trucks, horses, delivery robots, mobile carrying devices, low-speed vehicles, a train at a crossing) or needing a road the valley does not have (canyons, rural turnouts), and laws binding only a skateboard or moped rider, are outside it; they are listed with their reasons in `audit_and_scripts/excluded.jsonl`.

## Constraints checked

1. **Self-contained training set.** No training line references a test line. Test lines referencing training lines (allowed, new law builds on old): none.
2. **The test set introduces atoms.** An atom or enumeration value is *test-only* when every corpus line that mentions it — by the provenance document's statute references or by the keyword lexicon over the verbatim text — is a test line. The lists below are what the training ontology must not contain.
3. **Definitions follow their lines.** A definition used only by test lines, or whose term occurs in no training text, is in `example/test/definitions.jsonl`.

## What was rejected, and why

- School zones (NRS 484B.363(1)–(4)), which would have brought `dev.speed_limit_beacon`: training lines NRS 484B.650(1) and 484B.403(1) reference them as exceptions, so moving them breaks self-containment.
- Lane-control signals (NRS 484B.307(12)): referenced by NRS 484B.300(1) (obedience to devices).
- The white-cane stop (LVMC 11.30.140(B)) and the school-crossing signal (LVMC 11.46.030): referenced by the pedestrian-signal lines NRS 484B.283(1).
- Centre turn lanes (NRS 484B.223(2),(3)) and the bridge sight-line rule (484B.213(2)(c)): referenced by 484B.650(1), 484B.200(1) and 484B.270(2)(b).
- Safety zones (NRS 484B.110, .450(1)(h)): the pedestrian-signal lines 484B.283(1)(d) also name them, so the atom would not be test-only.
- Tour buses and the curb colour code (LVMC 11.52.030(A)): the colour code is the key to most municipal parking lines; moving it would gut the training parking corpus.
